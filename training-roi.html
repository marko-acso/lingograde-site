The measurement gap

You can measure almost every line in the L&D budget. Except this one.

Sales training has pipeline numbers. Safety training has incident rates. Language training has a smile from the provider and a self-reported "B2" on the employee's profile. When the budget review comes, there is nothing objective to point to.

The problem is not the training. The problem is that nobody put a ruler against it - before and after. That is the entire job LingoGrade does.

How the measurement works

A ruler at the start. A ruler at the end. The difference is your ROI.

1
Baseline, before training starts
Each employee has a relaxed 25-minute assessment. They receive an individual CEFR report; you receive a Team Summary. This is the "before" number - the one every later result is measured against.
2
Your provider trains. We stay out of it.
LingoGrade does not sell training. That is the point - an independent ruler has no incentive to flatter the result. Keep whatever provider or in-house programme you already run.
3
Re-measure after the training cycle
Same employee, same language, same rubric. Now you have before and after CEFR levels side by side - a board-ready answer to "did the budget move the level?"

Why an independent ruler

A provider grading its own homework is not a measurement.

When the same company sells the training and reports the result, the result is marketing. LingoGrade never sells training. Every assessment is run against the CEFR Companion Volume (Council of Europe, 2020) and signed, by name and date, by the assessor who conducted it.

You get a defensible, before-and-after CEFR record you can put in front of a CFO, a board, or an auditor - and it holds up because the party who measured it had nothing to sell you.
